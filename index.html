Climate change in the Philippines: more than an environmental crisis

◷ 6 min read•Core research topic

The Philippines faces climate-related risks including extreme rainfall, flooding, drought, rising temperatures, and sea-level rise. These hazards can affect ecosystems and infrastructure while also interrupting education, threatening health, and placing pressure on household livelihoods.

Understanding the issue requires both scientific evidence and attention to social vulnerability: people do not have equal resources to prepare, respond, and recover.

Climate change at a glance

Start with the essentials.

These are evidence-based concepts rather than unsourced headline statistics. Follow the source links to explore the underlying research.

🌡️

A warming planet

Human activities, especially greenhouse-gas emissions, are the main driver of the observed long-term warming described in climate science assessments.

🌧️

Risks are interconnected

Climate hazards can affect ecosystems, health, food and water security, infrastructure, education, and livelihoods—not just the weather.

🤝

Vulnerability is unequal

Exposure to hazards and the resources available to prepare and recover influence how severely people and communities are affected.

Sources: IPCC, Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability · PAGASA, Climate Change in the Philippines.

Science + Technology + Society

Knowledge helps communities prepare.

Scientific observations help us understand climate patterns and risks. Technology can help communicate warnings and support planning, but its effectiveness depends on access, preparedness, infrastructure, and public trust.

Observe and monitor

Satellites, weather stations, and scientific observations help track environmental conditions over time.

📡

Warn and communicate

Forecasts and warning systems can help people make informed decisions when messages are timely and accessible.

🗺️

Plan for risk

Hazard maps and climate projections can inform land-use decisions, preparedness, and infrastructure planning.

Reduce emissions

Real-world connections

Climate risks are lived locally.

These research themes show how climate hazards can connect to everyday life. They are framed as topics for evidence-based investigation, not claims about a single specific incident.

01

Coastal communities and sea-level rise

Coastal areas may face risks related to sea-level rise, storm surges, erosion, and damage to homes or sources of income. Local risk depends on geography and exposure.

02

Heavy rainfall, flooding, and daily life

Flooding can damage infrastructure, interrupt classes, affect transport, and create recovery costs for households and local services.

Children, health, and essential services

Climate and environmental risks can affect children's health, nutrition, education, protection, and access to safe water and sanitation.
